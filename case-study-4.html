Case Study 04

Accounts Payable Vendor Validation

Designed an automated accounts payable workflow that receives invoice information, validates vendors against an approved vendor master, applies invoice business rules, detects exceptions, and routes approved financial information into QuickBooks Online for accounts payable processing.

Project Overview

Accounts payable teams often spend significant time reviewing invoice information, confirming vendor status, checking purchase order requirements, identifying duplicate invoices, and determining whether an invoice is ready for processing.

This project demonstrates how automation can apply those controls consistently. Invoice data enters the workflow, vendor information is validated against an approved vendor master, invoice requirements are checked, and the transaction is routed either to an exception-review path or into QuickBooks Online for accounts payable processing.

Challenge

Reduce repetitive AP validation work while maintaining consistent vendor, invoice, and financial-system controls.

Solution

Build a Make.com workflow using webhooks, JSON, Google Sheets, business rules, routers, filters, QuickBooks Online, and Gmail notifications.

Outcome

Created a repeatable validation process that separates exceptions from approved invoices and prepares validated transactions for processing in QuickBooks Online.

Project Visual

The workflow below illustrates how invoice information enters the automation, moves through vendor and invoice validation, and is routed either to QuickBooks Online for processing or to an exception-review path.

How the Automation Works

  1. A custom webhook receives structured invoice information from the source process.
  2. JSON parsing identifies key invoice fields including Vendor ID, Vendor Name, Invoice Number, Invoice Date, Amount, Purchase Order, Payment Terms, and Vendor Email.
  3. Google Sheets searches the Vendor Master using the Vendor ID.
  4. The workflow validates whether the vendor exists and whether the vendor is currently active.
  5. Invoice information is checked for required fields and purchase order requirements.
  6. The Invoice Log is searched using Vendor ID and Invoice Number to identify potential duplicate invoices.
  7. A router applies the validation results and directs the invoice to either an approved path or an exception path.
  8. Approved invoice data is passed to the QuickBooks Online module for accounts payable processing.
  9. The approved transaction is recorded in the Invoice Log with its validation and processing status.
  10. Exceptions are logged with the appropriate reason and a Gmail notification is sent for review.

Business Rules & Validation Controls

The workflow applies several validation rules before an invoice can move to the QuickBooks Online processing path.

  • The Vendor ID must exist in the approved Vendor Master.
  • The vendor must have an Active status.
  • Required invoice information must be present.
  • If the vendor requires a purchase order, a PO number must be included.
  • Vendor ID and Invoice Number are checked against the Invoice Log to identify duplicate invoices.
  • Only invoices that pass all required validation rules are routed to the QuickBooks Online processing step.

QuickBooks Online Integration

QuickBooks Online is incorporated into the approved processing path so validated accounts payable information can move from the automation workflow into the accounting system.

Before financial information reaches QuickBooks, the workflow verifies vendor status, required invoice fields, purchase order requirements, and potential duplicate invoices. This helps keep validation logic upstream of the accounting-system step.

Integrating QuickBooks with Make.com demonstrates how operational workflows can connect financial controls with accounting-system processing while preserving an exception path for transactions that require human review.

Exception Handling

Not every invoice should move into QuickBooks without review. The workflow separates exceptions so they can be investigated before financial processing continues.

  • Vendor Not Found
  • Vendor Inactive
  • Missing Required Information
  • Missing Purchase Order
  • Duplicate Invoice

When an exception occurs, the workflow records the validation status and exception reason in the Invoice Log and sends a notification containing the information needed for follow-up.

Challenges & Problem Solving

One of the most important design considerations was determining which decisions could be automated safely and which conditions should be routed for human review before financial information reached QuickBooks Online.

Vendor validation required the workflow to compare incoming invoice information against a controlled vendor master rather than relying only on the information contained in the invoice.

Duplicate detection also required the workflow to search historical invoice records before approving a new transaction. Testing therefore included valid invoices, unknown vendors, inactive vendors, missing purchase orders, incomplete invoice data, and duplicate invoice numbers.

The QuickBooks integration added another important control point: transactions should reach the accounting system only after the required validation rules have been satisfied.

Separating approved transactions from exceptions creates a workflow that supports automation while preserving human review for conditions that require additional attention.

Business Value

  • Reduces repetitive manual vendor and invoice validation.
  • Applies accounts payable business rules more consistently.
  • Helps identify duplicate invoices before accounting-system processing.
  • Connects validated AP information with QuickBooks Online.
  • Improves visibility into invoices requiring additional review.
  • Creates a structured exception-handling process.
  • Provides a centralized invoice validation log for tracking and review.
  • Demonstrates how automation can support financial operations while maintaining validation controls.
